I am an engineer who likes problems that start out badly defined. Most of my
best work has begun with somebody saying "this is probably impossible, but…"
and ended with a system that quietly does the job every day without anyone
thinking about it.

## How I work

I care a lot about the seam between a system and the people using it. A
service that is technically correct but impossible to operate is not finished,
and neither is a tool that works only if you already know the answer. So I
spend more time than most on error messages, on documentation, and on the
question of what happens when this breaks at an inconvenient hour.

I write things down. Design documents before code, postmortems after
incidents, and the occasional blog post when something turns out to be more
interesting than expected.

## Away from the keyboard

I photograph things — mostly cities at the hours when nobody else is out, and
mostly on film when I can be bothered to develop it. There is a page of that
work on this site. It is not curated in any particular way; it is just what I
happened to be looking at.
